# Seven-day AI Creator Pilot

This follows the workflow in Mark Tilbury's *I Tried The LAZIEST Way to Make Money With AI*: research a few creator concepts, produce ten short videos for each, compare early reach, then make one small digital product for the strongest account. This file is a draft launch kit. It does not create social accounts, publish posts, accept platform terms, or spend money.

## What the video reported

Mark launched three Instagram personas in health, wealth, and relationships. He posted ten videos per account, then chose one account to lead the product test. The video reports 23 workbook sales at $12, or $276 revenue, and subtracts $99 for the video-generation plan and $19.52 in payment fees to report $157.48 profit. That is one creator's short experiment, not a result we can assume for a new account. The advertised tool discount was an affiliate offer.

## Pilot boundaries

- Three original fictional characters; every profile says it is an AI character.
- Ten original vertical videos per character; no copied scripts, clips, faces, voices, or testimonials.
- Track results for seven days; select a provisional winner using reach, watch time, saves, comments, profile visits, and link clicks, not views alone.
- Use free tools first. Do not start a paid trial until the user chooses a spending cap and approves the specific charge.
- No account creation, public posting, payment setup, or product publishing is included in this draft.
- Health content is general habit journaling, never diagnosis, treatment, supplement claims, or medical advice. Money content is basic budgeting, never investment advice or promises. Relationship content is general communication, never coercive tactics.

## The three characters

### Cal Mercer — practical everyday routines

An openly fictional AI neighbor in his late 70s who likes gardening, simple meals, walks, and routines that are easy to repeat. His tone is warm, dry, and unhurried. He shares ordinary habit ideas, not longevity claims or remedies.

**Bio:** `Fictional AI character | Small, ordinary routines for a steadier day | Not medical advice`

**Consistent visual prompt:** `Original fictional older man, late seventies, kind weathered face, short silver hair, soft plaid overshirt over a plain tee, friendly grounded expression, natural skin texture, candid documentary portrait, warm morning window light, modest home garden or kitchen, consistent face and clothing, vertical 9:16, no text, no logo, no resemblance to a real person.`

### Nora Vale — practical money habits

An openly fictional AI former shop owner in her 60s who talks about everyday spending choices, simple budgeting, and lessons from running a small shop. She does not recommend investments, promise wealth, or invent a real success story.

**Bio:** `Fictional AI character | Plain-language spending and small-business lessons | Educational, not financial advice`

**Consistent visual prompt:** `Original fictional woman in her mid-sixties, silver bob haircut, tasteful colorful scarf and simple jacket, confident amused expression, elegant but approachable, sitting at a small independent shop counter, natural skin texture, soft daylight, documentary lifestyle photography, consistent face and wardrobe, vertical 9:16, no luxury goods, no text, no logo, no resemblance to a real person.`

### Tessa Park — communication and relationships

An openly fictional AI in her late 20s who turns common misunderstandings into short, empathetic communication prompts. No fake dating stories presented as personal experience; no manipulation, diagnosis, or gender-baiting.

**Bio:** `Fictional AI character | Clearer conversations and kinder boundaries | Not a therapist`

**Consistent visual prompt:** `Original fictional woman in her late twenties, shoulder-length dark hair, casual modern clothing in muted colors, expressive but natural face, relaxed podcast-corner or apartment setting, warm natural light, candid editorial portrait, realistic skin texture, consistent face and wardrobe, vertical 9:16, no text, no logo, no resemblance to a real person.`

## Thirty short-video drafts

Keep each video around 15–25 seconds. Use a hook as on-screen text, one useful point, then a simple question. Add captions. If the generated video is only a few seconds long, use it as B-roll under a separately recorded or generated voiceover; do not force a talking avatar that looks unnatural.

### Cal: everyday routines

1. **Hook:** The habit trick I wish I learned sooner. **Script:** Make the first step almost too small to fail. Put the shoes by the door. Fill the water glass before bed. Make tomorrow easier for yourself tonight. **Question:** What tiny setup helps you?
2. **Hook:** You don't need a perfect morning routine. **Script:** Pick one thing you can repeat on an ordinary Tuesday. A short walk, opening the curtains, or eating breakfast at the table. Simple beats impressive. **Question:** Which one sounds doable?
3. **Hook:** A garden taught me this about progress. **Script:** You don't tug on a seed to make it grow. You give it light, water, and time. Habits work better when you make room for them instead of demanding instant results. **Question:** What are you giving time to?
4. **Hook:** Make the good choice the easy choice. **Script:** If you want to read more, leave the book where you sit. If you want to prep lunch, put the container on the counter. A little preparation can save a lot of willpower. **Question:** What could you set out tonight?
5. **Hook:** My no-fuss reset for a messy day. **Script:** Clear one small surface. Step outside for a minute. Write down the next useful thing. You don't have to fix the whole day at once. **Question:** What's your smallest reset?
6. **Hook:** Why I like repeating the same simple meal. **Script:** Less deciding can make a busy day feel lighter. Keep a few easy favorites in rotation, then change them when you feel like it. No special rules required. **Question:** What's your reliable meal?
7. **Hook:** A walk can be a pause, not a workout. **Script:** Leave the phone in your pocket for a few minutes. Notice the weather, the trees, the sound of the street. It doesn't need a step goal to count as a break. **Question:** Where do you like to walk?
8. **Hook:** One small thing to do before tomorrow. **Script:** Choose the first task, write it down, and put the note somewhere visible. A clear next step is kinder than a long list you won't look at. **Question:** What goes on your note?
9. **Hook:** The house feels calmer when this is ready. **Script:** I put tomorrow's keys, bag, and jacket in one place. It takes a moment and saves me the morning scavenger hunt. **Question:** What do you always misplace?
10. **Hook:** You can restart a routine without starting over. **Script:** Missed a day? Just do the next small version today. A routine is something you return to, not a streak you have to protect. **Question:** What would you like to return to?

### Nora: practical money habits

1. **Hook:** A budget isn't a punishment. **Script:** It's just a way to tell your money where you want it to go before the week gets busy. Start with the bills you know and one goal that matters to you. **Question:** What would you like to make room for?
2. **Hook:** Before buying it, I ask one question. **Script:** Where will I keep it, and when will I use it? If I can't answer either, I leave it in the cart for a day. That pause helps me choose on purpose. **Question:** What helps you avoid impulse buys?
3. **Hook:** The small-shop lesson that changed my ordering. **Script:** I stopped guessing and wrote down what actually sold. At home, the same idea works for subscriptions and repeat purchases: look at what you really use. **Question:** What did you forget you were paying for?
4. **Hook:** Try a one-week spending notebook. **Script:** No judgment and no fancy spreadsheet. Just write the date, what you bought, and whether it felt worth it. A little awareness can help you make your own next choice. **Question:** Would you try it?
5. **Hook:** Give your savings goal a name. **Script:** “Savings” feels vague. “Car repair buffer” or “December travel” tells you what you're protecting. A clear purpose can make a plan easier to stick with. **Question:** What would you call yours?
6. **Hook:** The subscription check I do every season. **Script:** Scan the last month of charges. Mark the ones you still use, the ones you meant to cancel, and the ones you share. Check the cancellation terms before changing anything. **Question:** Have you done a subscription check lately?
7. **Hook:** Cheap and useful are not the same thing. **Script:** I compare the full cost with how often I expect to use something. A low price isn't a bargain if it sits in a drawer. **Question:** What's the best useful thing you bought?
8. **Hook:** A simple way to plan a grocery trip. **Script:** Check what you already have, pick a few meals, then make the list. It won't make every week perfect, but it can cut down on buying duplicates. **Question:** List first or wander the aisles?
9. **Hook:** If a bill surprises you, save the details. **Script:** Note the due date, amount, and who to contact. Then look for the official provider options. Don't ignore a bill because it feels overwhelming. **Question:** What system keeps your paperwork straight?
10. **Hook:** One money conversation worth having at home. **Script:** Ask, “What's one expense we want to plan for this month?” No blame, just a shared heads-up and a chance to agree on priorities. **Question:** What would you plan for together?

### Tessa: communication and boundaries

1. **Hook:** A clear text can be kinder than a hint. **Script:** Try: “I want to see you. Are you free Thursday?” It says what you mean and gives the other person something clear to answer. **Question:** Do you prefer direct plans?
2. **Hook:** When plans change, try this sentence. **Script:** “I was looking forward to it. Can we pick a new day now?” It names how you feel and asks for a next step without turning it into a guessing game. **Question:** What helps you reschedule well?
3. **Hook:** A boundary doesn't need a courtroom argument. **Script:** “I can't make it tonight, but I hope you have a good time.” You can be warm and still be clear. **Question:** Is saying no hard for you?
4. **Hook:** Don't send the third follow-up in a panic. **Script:** Pause, do something else, and let the other person reply when they can. If a pattern bothers you, bring it up calmly when you're both available. **Question:** What helps you pause before texting?
5. **Hook:** A useful question after a disagreement. **Script:** “Do you want me to listen, or are we trying to solve it together?” It avoids jumping to advice when the other person wants to be heard. **Question:** Which do you usually need?
6. **Hook:** You can ask for clarity without accusing. **Script:** Try: “I might be reading this wrong. What did you mean when you said that?” Curiosity leaves room for an honest answer. **Question:** How do you clear up misunderstandings?
7. **Hook:** Being busy isn't a personality test. **Script:** People have different schedules and texting habits. Instead of guessing what a delay means, look at the pattern and talk about what kind of communication works for you. **Question:** What's your preferred check-in rhythm?
8. **Hook:** A small repair phrase I like. **Script:** “I see why that landed badly. Let me say it another way.” You don't have to win every sentence to keep a conversation respectful. **Question:** What's a phrase that helps repair a moment?
9. **Hook:** If you need time, say when you'll return. **Script:** “I'm getting overwhelmed. Can we take a break and talk after dinner?” A pause works better when the other person knows it's not an unexplained disappearance. **Question:** How do you ask for a pause?
10. **Hook:** Consistency is easier to notice than big promises. **Script:** Pay attention to whether plans, words, and actions line up over time. You don't have to decide everything from one perfect or terrible moment. **Question:** What does consistency mean to you?

## Production checklist

For each of the 30 drafts:

1. Generate one consistent character still using the matching visual prompt.
2. Create a vertical 9:16 scene or short B-roll clip that does not imitate a real person.
3. Add the original script as voiceover or readable captions, with clean audio and licensed music only.
4. Add an on-screen or caption disclosure such as `Fictional AI character` and use each platform's native AI label when required.
5. Review factual statements, spelling, visual continuity, and all asset rights before any post.
6. Save the draft and record the final prompt, tool, settings, and estimated cost.

## Seven-day test and scorecard

Post ten per account over the test window. Keep the cadence comparable across all three. Record each video's date, views/reach, average watch time or completion rate if shown, saves, comments, profile visits, follows, and link clicks. Do not call a winner based on one viral outlier alone. After the test, compare totals and per-post medians.

Before selling, create one small interactive workbook aligned with the leading account. Candidate shapes: Cal — a 30-day everyday-routine tracker; Nora — a spending-awareness workbook; Tessa — a conversation reflection journal. Review claims and usability, state clearly that the character is fictional and AI generated, and offer the product only after the user approves the final copy, price, platform, and any platform fees.

## External actions still requiring the account owner

The owner must create or choose the social accounts, review each public post, and personally handle account sign-in and payout/bank details. A paid subscription, free-trial conversion, product publication, or any charge requires the owner's explicit approval at that step. Platform AI labels must be selected during upload when required. Instagram's branded-content and AI-label options, and YouTube's altered/synthetic-content and monetization rules, may change; review the current upload controls before publishing.
